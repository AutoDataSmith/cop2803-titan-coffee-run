# Assignment 6 Notes to Professor

## AI Use Summary

Assignment 6 felt like a larger feature block than the earlier assignments because it added a full order and checkout flow on top of the existing login/session work. I used AI at the beginning to help make sure I was starting in the right direction instead of jumping straight into code.

AI helped me draft the Sprint 3 user story and acceptance criteria first. I reviewed the assignment requirements, compared them to my existing Assignment 5 project, and worked with AI to turn the requirements into a practical story for the Titan Coffee Run customer order flow.

AI also helped me create a step-by-step roadmap before implementation. We broke the work into smaller parts, including the order page, cart script, object-oriented classes, cart persistence, checkout page, totals, testing, and final documentation.

I created an Assignment 6 test case document early so I could test each feature before moving to the next one. As I tested the order page, cart updates, remove buttons, redirects, and quantity validation, I recorded the results instead of waiting until the end.

For checkout, I originally thought I might need a more complete checkout process. After rereading the assignment, I kept the checkout page focused on the actual requirement: showing the cart items and calculating the final total.

AI was used mostly as a planning, review, and debugging partner. I used it to help organize the work, identify grading risks, explain implementation choices, and refine small bugs found during testing.

I tried to keep the code practical for this class project. When I noticed extra polish items, such as duplicate cart rows for the same product, I documented them separately instead of turning every observation into extra scope.
